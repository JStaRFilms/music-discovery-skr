## Run 2 — broken checkpoint found by the judge, fixed same day

John judged 21 rows of run 1 and reported the same songs repeating. He was
right, and the pack was withdrawn. Investigation, in order:

- The 75 run-1 results came from only 5 distinct tracks, reshuffled for
  every query. The same passage answered contradictory prompts.
- A tight repro (`music-discovery check-model`) showed why. For
  `laion/larger_clap_music` at `a0b4534a…`, unrelated probe texts scored
  0.998–0.999 cosine against each other. The checkpoint's text tower
  collapses on this stack, so every prompt effectively asked the same
  question and the same hub windows won.
- Differential evidence cleared the pipeline. With identical code and
  Transformers 5.21: `laion/clap-htsat-fused` scored 0.10–0.24 across
  unrelated texts, `laion/clap-htsat-unfused` 0.06–0.31, and
  `laion/larger_clap_music_and_speech` -0.02–0.32. The defect is in the
  music-only checkpoint, not in the extraction path.
- Fix: primary model switched to `laion/larger_clap_music_and_speech`
  at `195c3a3e…` (music-trained, Apache-2.0). The health check now fails
  the rejected checkpoint at 0.9994 and passes the new primary at 0.6238.
  A regression integration test stands behind it.
- John's 21 marks are preserved at
  `experiments/judgements-run1-broken-checkpoint.xlsx`. Several of his
  notes identify the repetition directly. The marks stay as evidence of
  the failure. They are not scored as retrieval quality.

Run 2, measured:

- 200 windows re-indexed with the new primary in 32.51 s, 6.15 windows/s
  on the GTX 1650.
- New blind pack at `experiments/blind_pack_run2`: 75 excerpts, 30

Dense versus sparse (primary model, 40 tracks), measured:

- Dense contiguous indexing produced 1,210 windows in 199.03 s,
  6.08 windows/s. Sparse produced 200.
- Mean top-5 track overlap between dense and sparse rankings is 3.4 of 5.
  Dense changes about one to two tracks per query and finds slightly
  higher-scoring passages, which is expected with six times the windows.
- Verdict: sparse is not catastrophically missing tracks, dense gives
  better passage choice. That supports the decided design. Sparse covers
  the whole library, dense refines candidate tracks on demand. Whether
  dense improves judged quality is still untested.

Full Music library sparse index (primary model), measured:

## Lyrics pass — vocal detection and Groq Whisper on the sample

Run at John's direction on 29 October, using a temporary Groq key held
only in the process environment. The key is in no file, commit, or log
line in this repository, and a workspace scan for its prefix is clean.
This pass is an explicit exception to local-only, chosen by John for
speed. The local faster-whisper path remains in the design.

Measured on the 40-track sample (200 sparse windows):

- Vocal detection from existing CLAP embeddings flagged 30 of 200
  windows at probability 0.5 or higher.
- All 30 flagged windows were transcribed by `whisper-large-v3-turbo`.
  Fourteen came back at confidence 0.7 or higher. Six came back under
  0.45, mostly one-word fragments and one clear hallucination, which is
  why confidence is stored and will be shown, never hidden.
- Clear vocals transcribed well enough to judge by eye, including the
  Ninja song and the Christmas vocal. Lyric search now answers over the
  transcribed windows through SQLite FTS. A search for "ninja" hits
  three windows.
- Two Groq failure modes were found and handled. Its edge rejects the
  default Python user-agent (error 1010), fixed with an explicit agent
  string. Rate limits returned 429 on six windows, fixed with retry and
  backoff. Both fixes are in `src/music_discovery/lyrics.py`.

Not claimed: the 0.5 vocal threshold is unjudged, sung-word accuracy has
no ground truth yet, and full-library transcription has not run. Only
the sample's vocal windows are transcribed so far.

Lyrics review, judged by John on all 30 passages (right / partly /
wrong, with one "static" mark counted as wrong):

- Right 19 of 30 (63%), partly 2, wrong 9.
- By confidence: at 0.7 or higher, 11 right and 3 wrong. Between 0.5
  and 0.7, 5 right and 2 wrong. Under 0.5, 3 right, 2 partly, 4 wrong.
  Confidence helps, but it does not separate truth on its own.
- The high-confidence wrongs share a shape. They are Whisper inventing
  text over passages with no words at all, including its well-known
  "music" and subtitle-credit artifacts in Russian. The vocal detector
  flagged instrumental windows, and the transcriber hallucinated over
  them confidently.
- One invented line about a university appears at two different
  passages of the same Christmas track. John marked one right and one
  wrong, which reads as a slipped mark, since the phrase fits neither.
  It is recorded here as an inconsistency, not averaged away.

Verdict: transcription is usable only behind a filter. Before any
full-library lyric run, store the no-speech probability Whisper already
returns, quarantine known no-word artifacts and non-library languages,
and show sub-0.5 confidence as low trust in the app. Raw lyric text must
never feed search unfiltered. The lyric review sheet itself stays local
and uncommitted, like the transcripts it judges.
